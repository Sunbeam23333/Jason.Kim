Omni IIE Bench

Benchmarking the Practical Capabilities of Image Editing Models. Omni IIE Bench tests whether an editor can apply the requested change at multiple semantic scales, preserve unrelated content, and coordinate a long sequence of edits without compounding errors.

CVPR 2026 paper Single-turn consistency Multi-turn coordination Human-curated benchmark
My role

Co-author (sixth author). The page reflects the paper’s benchmark construction and evaluation protocol without claiming equal-contribution status.

Benchmark scale

A dual-track evaluation built from 2,856 final images

1,725 Single-turn editing dialogues across semantic scales.
260 Multi-turn groups containing 1,131 edit turns.
12 Source datasets spanning diverse visual domains.
Track 1

Single-turn Consistency

A single edit is tested at low and high semantic scales. The benchmark asks whether the model changes the intended attribute or entity while keeping foreground and background regions that should remain stable.

  • Attribute modification and entity replacement.
  • Localized foreground/background fidelity.
  • Instruction compliance under different edit granularities.
Track 2

Multi-turn Coordination

Edit sequences alternate semantic scales for as many as 16 turns. This exposes error accumulation, instruction conflicts, and the model’s ability to preserve earlier successful edits while applying a new request.

  • Up to 16 sequential turns.
  • Alternating fine- and coarse-grained edits.
  • Coordination across the entire editing history.
Data curation

Automated generation followed by strict human filtering

The construction pipeline uses GPT-4o to create editing instructions, Nano Banana to synthesize candidate edits, and GroundingDINO plus SAM to localize relevant regions. Human filtering then removes poor edits and unreliable masks before evaluation.

12 source datasets Instruction & image generation GroundingDINO + SAM masks Multi-stage human review
\[ M_{\mathrm{fg}}=M_{\mathrm{gt}}, \qquad M_{\mathrm{bg}}=1-M_{\mathrm{gt}}. \]
Ground-truth masks define the edited foreground and its complementary background so fidelity can be measured region by region.
Evaluation

Regional fidelity, global quality, and one-strike instruction compliance

LPIPS and CLIP are computed over foreground, background, and the whole image. SSIM measures structural preservation, while a one-strike QA term penalizes failure to satisfy the requested edit. PSNR is also reported by the benchmark, but it is not part of the paper’s Overall score.

\[ \begin{aligned} \mathrm{Overall} &=\frac14\Bigg( \frac{3-\sum_{r\in\{\mathrm{FG},\mathrm{BG},\mathrm{ALL}\}}\mathrm{LPIPS}_r}{3}\\ &\qquad +\frac{\sum_{r\in\{\mathrm{FG},\mathrm{BG},\mathrm{ALL}\}}\mathrm{CLIP}_r}{3}\\ &\qquad+\mathrm{QA}+\mathrm{SSIM} \Bigg). \end{aligned} \]
Lower LPIPS is converted to a positive contribution; CLIP, QA, and SSIM reward semantic fidelity, compliance, and structure preservation.
Why it matters

A practical editor must coordinate change and preservation

Beyond aesthetics

A visually pleasing image can still fail the instruction or alter protected content.

Beyond one turn

Long edit sessions reveal compounding errors hidden by isolated single-step tests.

Actionable diagnosis

Foreground/background scores identify where an editor changes too much or too little.
