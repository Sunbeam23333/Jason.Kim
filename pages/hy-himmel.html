HY-Himmel Technical Report

Hierarchical Interleaved Multi-stream Motion Encoding for Long Video Understanding. HY-Himmel separates sparse semantic anchors from dense compressed-domain motion so a multimodal language model can reason over long videos without paying the visual-token cost of uniformly encoding every frame.

arXiv 2605.08158 RGB / Motion Vector / Residual Frozen VLM Video-MME
My role

First author and project lead; joint corresponding author with Hongzhu Yi. I led the hierarchical semantic-motion design, training formulation, and evaluation.

Outcome

More temporal evidence with a much smaller context

63.5 Video-MME score reported by HY-Himmel.
+2.3 Points over the dense 32-frame baseline (61.2).
3.6× Fewer context tokens than the dense-frame setting.

The central result is not simply “sample fewer frames.” HY-Himmel assigns different jobs to different streams: sparse anchors carry scene semantics, while compressed video signals preserve local motion at low token cost.

Problem formulation

Decompose a long video into semantic anchors and interleaved motion clips

Uniform frame sampling wastes visual tokens on redundant appearance, yet aggressive sparse sampling can miss brief actions. HY-Himmel first selects semantic anchor frames and then associates them with local interleaved clips that retain fine-grained temporal evidence.

\[ \begin{aligned} \mathcal{V}&=\{x_1,\ldots,x_T\}\\ &\longrightarrow\{x_t:t\in\mathcal{A}\}\\ &\qquad\cup\{\mathcal{I}_k:k\in[1,K]\}. \end{aligned} \]
\(\mathcal{A}\) indexes sparse semantic anchors; each \(\mathcal{I}_k\) is a local interleaved clip around an anchor.
Long input video Semantic I-frame anchors Local RGB / MV / residual clips Interleaved VLM tokens
Semantic path

Sparse global understanding

  • I-frames capture objects, scenes, and high-level events with the full visual encoder.
  • Sparse placement controls the expensive ViT budget over long durations.
  • Anchor semantics provide the reference space used to align motion features.
Motion path

Dense local dynamics

  • RGB clips preserve local appearance around an anchor.
  • Motion vectors encode displacement already available in compressed video.
  • Residual maps retain changes that motion compensation cannot explain.
Adaptive fusion

Gate motion vectors and residuals before combining them with RGB

Motion vectors are efficient but imperfect; residuals expose newly appearing details and compensation errors. A learned gate decides how much each compressed-domain signal should contribute for a local clip.

\[ \begin{aligned} g_k^{mr} &=\sigma\!\left(W_g[h_k^{mv};h_k^{res}]+b_g\right),\\ h_k^{mr} &=g_k^{mr}\odot h_k^{mv}\\ &\quad +(1-g_k^{mr})\odot h_k^{res}. \end{aligned} \]
\(h_k^{mv}\) and \(h_k^{res}\) are the motion-vector and residual representations; \(g_k^{mr}\) is the learned element-wise gate.
Training

Align motion with semantics, then inject compact motion tokens

Training first places compressed-domain motion representations in the semantic space of the visual model. Contrastive and cosine terms encourage agreement without erasing complementary motion information.

\[ \mathcal L_{\mathrm{align}} =\mathcal L_{\mathrm{InfoNCE}} +\lambda_{\cos}\bigl(1-\cos(m_k,v_k)\bigr). \]

The aligned motion tokens are then inserted at designated positions while the original embedding sequence is protected with stop-gradient. This keeps the pretrained VLM stable and makes the additional temporal pathway explicit.

\[ E'=\operatorname{sg}(E)\odot \left(1-\Pi\mathbf 1_{KK_m}\right)+\Pi M. \]
\(M\) contains learned motion tokens and \(\Pi\) maps them into the interleaved sequence; \(\operatorname{sg}\) denotes stop-gradient.
Interpretation

What the technical report establishes—and what it does not

Established

Compressed-domain motion can complement sparse semantic frames and improve long-video QA under a substantially smaller context budget.

Design implication

Token allocation should follow information density: full semantic encoding where it matters, low-cost motion encoding where temporal coverage matters.

Boundary

The approach depends on compressed-video signals and inherits errors from motion estimation, anchor selection, and the frozen visual-language backbone.
